Frost risk is highest on clear, calm nights when minimum temperature is forecast below 4°C, since radiative cooling is strongest without cloud cover or wind mixing. Light irrigation before a frost night raises the effective heat capacity of the soil and can reduce crop canopy temperature drop by 1-2°C.
Heat stress above 35°C during flowering causes pollen sterility and flower drop in many crops (wheat, tomato, mustard), directly reducing yield even without visible wilting. Light, frequent irrigation and avoiding top-dressing nitrogen right before a heat spike (which increases transpiration demand) both help.
Mulching reduces both frost severity (insulation) and heat stress (reduced soil temperature swing and evaporation), making it a low-cost practice worth recommending across both risk types.
