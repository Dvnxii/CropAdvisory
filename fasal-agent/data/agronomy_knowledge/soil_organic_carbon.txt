Soil organic carbon (SOC) is a primary indicator of long-term soil health: it improves water-holding capacity, nutrient cycling, microbial activity, and soil structure. Indian agricultural soils commonly range from below 0.5% SOC (low, degraded) to above 1.5% (high, well-managed).

Practices that raise SOC over 3-5 years: incorporating crop residue instead of burning it, green manuring with legumes (dhaincha, sunhemp), farmyard manure or compost application, reduced tillage, and cover cropping in fallow periods.

Low SOC soils typically also show poor nitrogen-use efficiency, since microbial nitrogen cycling depends on organic matter; SOC improvement and fertilizer efficiency should be addressed together rather than treating fertilizer dose as the only lever.
